# Wikipedia Notability Requirements - Guidance

## Understanding the Notability Message

The message in the sandbox template is a standard Wikipedia **Articles for Creation (AfC)** rejection notice that appears when:

1. **Insufficient independent sources** - No published articles, books, or academic papers about the subject
2. **Lack of significant coverage** - Sources only mention the person in passing, not detailed coverage
3. **Primary sources only** - Information comes from resume, personal website, or company profiles

## Wikipedia Notability Criteria for People

To have a Wikipedia article, you need to meet the [General Notability Guideline (GNG)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Notability):

> **"Significant coverage in reliable sources that are independent of the subject"**

### What This Means

#### ✅ ACCEPTABLE Sources (Secondary, Independent):
- **News articles** in major newspapers, magazines, or trade publications about you
- **Academic papers** that discuss your work or cite your research extensively
- **Books** that dedicate sections to your contributions
- **Industry publications** with feature articles about your projects
- **Conference proceedings** where you're a keynote speaker (with coverage)
- **Technical journals** reviewing your software or methodologies

#### ❌ NOT ACCEPTABLE Sources (Primary, Dependent, or Insufficient):
- Your personal website or portfolio (emanuelecannizzaro.github.io)
- Company profiles or press releases from Airbus
- Your LinkedIn, GitHub, or social media profiles
- Conference attendance without coverage (just being a participant)
- Self-published articles or blog posts
- Resume or CV information
- Mentions in passing (e.g., "team member Emanuele Cannizzaro")

## Types of Coverage That Establish Notability

### For Engineers and Technical Professionals:

1. **Major Innovation or Invention**
   - Patents that received significant industry attention
   - Software tools adopted widely in industry
   - Novel methodologies that changed industry practice
   - Coverage: "Engineer Develops Revolutionary Digital Twin System for Aerospace Industry"

2. **Industry Recognition**
   - Major awards from professional organizations
   - Keynote speeches at major international conferences
   - Industry leadership positions with coverage
   - Coverage: "Airbus Engineer Wins Aerospace Innovation Award for Fatigue Analysis System"

3. **Academic Impact**
   - Highly-cited research papers (>100 citations)
   - Academic positions or fellowships
   - Published books on technical subjects
   - Coverage: Multiple academic papers citing your methodology

4. **Media Coverage**
   - Feature articles in trade magazines (Aviation Week, Aerospace Engineering)
   - Interviews in technical publications
   - Coverage of your specific projects in industry news
   - Coverage: "Inside Airbus's New Digital Twin Platform with Lead Engineer"

5. **Open Source Impact**
   - Founding major open-source projects with significant adoption
   - Core maintainer of widely-used projects with press coverage
   - Coverage: "Top 10 Open Source Projects in Aerospace Engineering"
